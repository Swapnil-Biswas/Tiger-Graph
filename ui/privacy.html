Privacy & Regulatory Data Protection Policy

Last Updated: September 23, 2026 • Compliant with FinCEN 31 CFR § 1020.320 & GDPR Art. 22

1. Ingestion Scope & PII Tokenization

The TigerGraph Fraud Defense platform processes transaction graphs, card identifiers, hardware signatures, and geolocation records strictly for the purpose of financial crime detection, AML structuring detection, and threat neutralization. Personally Identifiable Information (PII) is subject to irreversible hashing and role-based cryptographic tokenization prior to ingress into GraphStore layers.

2. Dynamic Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) & Masking

Access to raw customer account data, full primary account numbers (PAN), and device telemetry is governed by four strict RBAC clearance tiers:

  • Tier L1 (Fraud Analyst): View case summaries, masked card numbers (e.g. C12382-**), and trigger verification inquiries.
  • Tier L2 (Senior Fraud Ops): Authorize high-exposure mitigations (≥ $1,000), examine unmasked multi-card nexuses, and approve SAR filings.
  • AML Compliance Officer: Review FinCEN Form 111 XML payloads, override jurisdictional thresholds, and execute regulatory submissions.
  • Chief Risk Officer: Executive governance, model drift auditing, and policy threshold reconfiguration.

3. Cryptographic Chain-of-Custody & Audit Trail

Every API request, agent reasoning step, consensus vote, and human override generates an immutable entry in the cryptographic audit ledger. Each block contains an SHA-256 hash of the preceding record, a UTC timestamp, investigator identity, and an HMAC digital signature, guaranteeing non-repudiation under FRE 902.

4. Five-Year Mandatory Statutory Retention

Pursuant to 31 CFR § 1020.320(d), all supporting documentation relating to filed SARs and investigated case dossiers must be retained for a minimum period of five (5) years from the date of filing. The platform enforces immutable retention locks preventing unauthorized record expungement during the statutory window.

5. Cross-Border Transfers & FATF Corridor Compliance

Transaction hops spanning international jurisdictions are scrutinized against Financial Action Task Force (FATF) high-risk corridor matrices. Cross-border correspondent banking analysis automatically flags geographic mismatches and multi-jurisdiction structuring schemes.
