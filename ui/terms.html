1. Scope and Autonomous System Operation

These Terms of Service govern the access and deployment of the TigerGraph Autonomous Fraud Investigator & Next-Best-Action platform (“the System”). The System utilizes a three-agent cognitive swarm (AML Specialist, Cyber Forensics Agent, and Orchestrator) interacting with multi-hop GSQL query engines and empirical Bayesian uncertainty models to score, contextualize, and recommend mitigations for financial fraud.

2. Dual-Gate Action Policy Interception (Rules R1–R10)

All autonomous swarm outputs are subject to deterministic interception by the Dual-Gate Policy Engine. The System guarantees that no machine learning or large language model component possesses uninhibited authority to execute punitive card blocks, account terminations, or merchant blacklistings. Actions are evaluated against Rules R1 through R10, ensuring statutory compliance, weak-signal safeguards, and mandatory human escalation for high-exposure thresholds.

3. Human-in-the-Loop Analyst Override Protocol

In compliance with European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) Article 22, United States Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA), and Basel Committee Banking Governance Guidelines, certified human analysts retain full authority to override machine verdicts. Overrides require structured justification, role authentication (Tier L1 or Tier L2), and are permanently recorded in the cryptographic audit ledger.

4. Statutory Reporting & FinCEN Disclosures

Automated generation of electronic FinCEN Form 111 XML 2.0 Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR) does not relieve the deploying institution of its statutory obligations under 31 CFR § 1020.320. Financial institutions must designate authorized compliance personnel to review, verify, and electronically transmit XML filings to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network.

5. Evidentiary Certification under Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 902

Evidentiary bundles sealed by the System employ SHA-256 Merkle tree root hashes and HMAC-SHA256 signatures designed to satisfy the self-authentication criteria established under Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE) Rule 902(13) (Certified Records Generated by an Electronic Process) and Rule 902(14) (Certified Data from Electronic Devices). Any bit-flip or manual modification immediately invalidates the Merkle proof.
